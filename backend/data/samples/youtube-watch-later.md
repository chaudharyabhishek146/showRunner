# YouTube — Search and Watch Later

A sample product document. Upload one like this, say which flow you want shown,
and point the agent at your YouTube tab.

## What this product does

YouTube is a video platform with two very different ways in. Most people arrive
through the **home feed**, which is recommendation-driven: the platform decides
what you see. The rest of the product exists for the moments when that isn't
what you want — when you have a specific thing in mind, or something to come
back to later.

**Search** is the deliberate path. You type what you're after and the platform
gets out of the way.

**Watch Later** is the personal queue. It exists because the moment you *find*
something worth watching is rarely the moment you have time to watch it.

## The flow this walkthrough demonstrates

1. **Start on the home feed.** This is the default surface, and the baseline the
   rest of the flow is contrasted against — everything here was chosen for you.

2. **Move to the search box.** It sits at the top of every page in the product,
   which is the point: intent is never more than one click away, no matter how
   deep into a video you are.

3. **Search for a specific topic.** The results are the same catalogue as the
   home feed, ordered by what you asked for rather than by what the platform
   predicts. This is the moment the viewer takes control.

4. **Open the guide.** The hamburger at the top left expands the navigation
   rail. Everything personal to the viewer lives here — subscriptions, history,
   and the saved queue — rather than being scattered through the product.

5. **Show where the saved list lives.** Watch Later sits under **You**,
   alongside subscriptions and history. It is one click from anywhere on the
   site, which is what makes saving worth doing at all.

## Concepts worth explaining out loud

**Saving is one gesture.** From a search result or a video page, a viewer adds
to the queue without leaving what they're doing. No bookmarking, no second app,
no "I'll remember to look this up later" — which is the failure mode the queue
is designed to replace.

**The queue is a list, not a folder.** There is nothing to organise, name, or
file. Videos go in when you find them and leave when you've watched them. The
absence of structure is deliberate: any structure at all is a tax paid at the
exact moment the viewer is least willing to pay it.

**Discovery and intent are the same catalogue.** The home feed, search, and the
saved queue are three views of one library. A viewer moves between them without
ever leaving the product, which is why the queue fills up in the first place.

## Questions this document can answer

- Why is the search box on every page rather than on a dedicated screen?
- What is the difference between the home feed and search results?
- Where does a saved video actually go, and how does a viewer get back to it?

## Questions it deliberately does not

Cross-device sync, offline downloads, playlist sharing, and anything about
Premium are out of scope for this flow. If a viewer asks, the honest answer is
that the document doesn't cover it — the agent will say exactly that rather than
guess, which is the behaviour you want in front of a customer.
